Insurance companies profit when you take less. We don't get paid unless you win — so we front every cost and handle every call. That is the whole arrangement, and it is worth understanding exactly, because "no fee" gets used loosely in this industry.

What it means in practice

There is no retainer to sign a check for, no hourly rate, and no invoice while your case is open. We are paid a percentage of what we recover for you. If we recover nothing, we are paid nothing, and you do not owe us a fee.

Who pays the case costs

A personal injury case costs money to build: court filing fees, police and medical records, accident reconstruction, deposition transcripts, expert witnesses. The firm advances all of it. You are never asked for money up front to move your case forward.

Case costs are separate from the attorney's fee, and we explain both in writing before you sign anything. There are no surprises at the end, because the numbers were on the table at the beginning.

Why firms work this way

Because the alternative would put lawyers out of reach for exactly the people who need them most. Someone who has just been hit by a car, cannot work, and is watching medical bills arrive does not have thousands of dollars for a retainer. The contingency fee exists so that the person with the injury and the insurance company with the lawyers start on something closer to level ground.

It also aligns us with you. We do better when you do better. An insurer's adjuster is paid to close your claim cheaply; we are paid only if your claim closes well.

The questions people actually ask

What if I fired my old lawyer? You can change attorneys. Any fee owed to a prior firm is typically resolved out of the same recovery, not out of your pocket.

What if we lose? You do not owe an attorney's fee. We will walk you through how case costs are handled in that situation before you sign, so you know where you stand.

Is the consultation really free? Yes. Calling us costs nothing and commits you to nothing. If you don't have a case, we'll tell you that and you'll have lost a phone call.
