The first offer is sized for someone who doesn't have a lawyer yet. Maximizing what you recover is not a trick or an argument — it is the unglamorous work of documenting every category of loss the law allows and refusing to close the file before the medical picture is complete.

What California lets you recover

Medical expenses. Not only the bills you have already received, but the cost of treatment you still need. Future care is a real category of damages and it is the one adjusters most want to leave out.

Lost income. Wages you missed, and if the injury limits what you can do going forward, your reduced earning capacity.

Pain and suffering. Physical pain, and the loss of the things you used to be able to do. This is often the largest component and the one with the widest negotiating range.

Property damage. Repair or replacement of your vehicle and anything in it.

Where money gets left behind

Settling before you finish treating. Once you sign a release, the case is closed permanently — including for the surgery nobody knew you would need. This is the single most expensive mistake people make.

Gaps in treatment. Waiting weeks to see a doctor gives the insurer its favorite argument: that you were not badly hurt. See someone promptly and follow the treatment plan.

Missing a policy. The at-fault driver's liability coverage is often not the only money available. Your own underinsured motorist coverage, a rideshare company's commercial policy, an employer's policy if the other driver was working — these get missed constantly.

Accepting their fault percentage. California uses pure comparative fault, so being partly at fault reduces your recovery rather than eliminating it. The adjuster's proposed percentage is an opening position, not a finding.

The recorded statement. Given while you are on pain medication and before anyone knows the extent of your injuries, it exists to produce quotes that get used against you later.

What actually moves the number

Complete medical documentation, every policy identified, liability established with evidence rather than argument, and a file built as though it is going to trial. Insurers track which firms try cases and which ones always settle. That knowledge is priced into every offer they make you.
