Crashes don't keep office hours. You can reach a real person about your case at any hour, any day — nights, weekends and holidays included. Not a recording promising a callback. Not a chatbot. A person who takes down what happened and gets a lawyer moving on it.

Why the hour you call actually matters

The evidence that decides these cases has a shelf life measured in days, not months. Intersection cameras and store security systems overwrite their footage on a loop, often within a week. Witnesses who were happy to talk at the scene stop answering unknown numbers. The other driver's account firms up once they have spoken to their own insurer.

Meanwhile, the insurance company is already moving. Adjusters are frequently assigned within hours of a reported crash. Calling at 11pm on a Saturday instead of waiting until Monday morning can be the difference between having the video and arguing without it.

What happens on the call

We ask what happened, where, when, and whether you have seen a doctor. That is usually five or ten minutes. If it's the middle of the night, we are not going to keep you on the phone rebuilding the accident — we take enough to start preserving evidence and to know how urgent your situation is, and a lawyer follows up.

If there is a deadline problem, a government claim that needs filing, or footage that has to be preserved immediately, that starts the same night rather than three days later.

English and Spanish, around the clock

Bilingual intake is not a daytime-only feature here. Whether you call at noon or at 3am, you can explain what happened in English or in Spanish, to a person rather than a translation line. Ismael Noriega is on the team specifically so Spanish-speaking clients are never handed a form and left to work it out alone.

What it costs

Nothing. The case review is free whether you call at 2pm or 2am, it does not obligate you to hire us, and there is no fee unless we win. If you don't have a case, we'll tell you that on the call.

What to do before you call

If you can, take photographs of the vehicles, the scene, and your injuries. Get the other driver's insurance information and the names of any witnesses. Do not give a recorded statement to the other side's insurer, and do not accept or cash a settlement check. Then call — the rest we can handle.
